# Characters, city population and vehicles

This is the implementation record for the Kenney character/vehicle expansion: what was imported,
what is deliberately **not** used, how NPCs and vehicles work inside TheCity, and what was actually
tested. The visual asset catalogue is documented separately in
[KENNEY-ASSET-LIBRARY.md](KENNEY-ASSET-LIBRARY.md).

---

## 1. What shipped

| Area | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Character assets inspected | 51 classified humanoid/accessibility records from 6 character-bearing kits |
| Player avatars | **Unchanged** custom LoftLife rig; no imported model is player-compatible |
| NPC population | 50 deterministic agents on high, 34 medium, 20 low |
| NPC rendering | Full animated rigs near the player, static Kenney pedestrians in the middle distance, nothing far away |
| Animation states | 13 shared states, server-validated, distance-driven gait |
| Vehicle catalogue | 134 imported models in 12 groups, with real previews |
| Live city fleet | Procedural cars (including 4 ambient taxis), 4 buses, 2 three-car metro trains, 6 parked cars |
| Transit | Existing server-owned LoftLine routes, taxi hailing, rail and 15 taxi destinations preserved |
| Free driving | **Not added**; vehicle enter/exit animation and interaction points are ready for it |

The integration is live in the world, not a preview-only gallery: `buildMainWorld` creates the crowd
and traffic systems, `RoomViewport` updates them every frame, and the existing server shares player
animation state.

## 3. Player avatars remain the custom system

No player model was replaced. The following established behaviour is untouched and still covered by
the existing acceptance tests:

- foundational facial features change only through the paid **Character Makeover**;
- hair and makeup change at the home bathroom mirror or a salon;
- clothing is browsed and purchased in shops and enters inventory;
- outfit changes happen at the home closet, with unlimited saved outfits;
- the procedural rig, wardrobe geometry, live preview and emotes all remain authoritative.

Imported characters are only used for the **distant** NPC tier, where a static silhouette is enough.
Nearby NPCs use `createAvatarRig` with generated `AvatarConfig`s, so they share the player's skeleton,
proportions, clothing layers and animation vocabulary. That is what keeps the crowd visually
consistent with players instead of switching to a different art style at conversation distance.

## 4. Animation foundation

`shared/animation.mjs` is the single vocabulary shared by the client and server:

`idle`, `walk`, `run`, `stand`, `sit`, `wave`, `dance`, `cheer`, `talk`, `gesture`, `jump`,
`enter_vehicle`, `exit_vehicle`.

- `normalizeAnimation` rejects anything outside that set at the realtime boundary, so a modified
  client cannot push arbitrary animation labels to other players.
- `animationForPose` derives walk/run/sit/vehicle poses from speed, sprint, seating and vehicle state.
- `stridePhaseStep(speed, delta)` advances the gait by **distance travelled**, not a fixed timer. At
  walking and sprinting speeds the feet stay planted instead of sliding.
- `turnLeanFor` adds a small, clamped torso lean while turning.
- `src/game/avatar.ts` blends every pose with an exponential smoothing factor (`1 - exp(-12 * dt)`),
  so idle → walk → run → sit → stand transitions ease rather than snap.
- `enter_vehicle` and `exit_vehicle` have dedicated crouch/lean poses for future player driving.

Multiplayer: `App.tsx` derives the local state from actual pose speed, `RoomViewport` estimates remote
speed and sends the full state set, and `server/realtime.mjs` accepts only normalized states. Remote
avatars are rendered with the same rig and animation function as the local player.

**Honest limitation:** the Kenney kits contain no skeleton, skinning or animation clips. The
movement quality comes from the existing LoftLife procedural rig, not from imported animation data.

## 5. City population

`shared/city-crowd.mjs` is a pure, deterministic simulation (no Three.js, DOM or network), rendered by
`src/game/cityCrowd.ts`.

### Behaviour

- Agents walk the **sidewalk ring** of a real city block, inset into the sidewalk band. Building
  footprints are never crossed.
- Street crossings happen only at block corners, where the city paints crosswalks, and connect to the
  matching corner of the adjacent block.
- Roles: pedestrians, sitters, loiterers and workers.
  - sitters walk to a real venue seat, sit for 45–120 s, then stand and leave;
  - workers enter a venue through its door, stand at an interior point, talk/idle, then leave;
  - loiterers wait near venue doors, sometimes step inside;
  - pedestrians loop blocks and occasionally visit a venue.
- Nearby players are noticed within 3.3 m: the agent stops, turns to face the player and waves (or
  talks, for staff/sitters), then resumes with a cooldown.
- Separation keeps agents from overlapping each other or the player.
- The population is seeded, so every device gets the same city for the same seed.

### Detail budgets

| Quality | Crowd | Rig avatars | Static props | Prop range | Rig range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| High | 50 | 8 | 22 | 155 m | 44 m |
| Medium | 34 | 5 | 14 | 120 m | 34 m |
| Low | 20 | 3 | 8 | 88 m | 24 m |

`assignCrowdDetail` sorts by distance and assigns rig → prop → hidden. Far agents are frozen, not
simulated. Graphics quality changes cap the crowd immediately; renderer resolution and shadows settle
on the next world entry.

### Recycling and fast travel

Hidden agents are rehomed near the player every 1.4 s (two at a time) so the population follows a
walking player without simulating the whole city. A map/taxi/airport jump over 80 m rehomes the whole
hidden population at once. Recycled agents spawn **20–100 m** from the player — far enough not to pop
in beside the camera, close enough to keep the next street busy. Rehoming preserves each agent's role
when the new district supports it, so a long trip does not turn every worker and sitter into a
pedestrian.

The real-city check reports 30 agents, 0 sidewalk violations, 7,636 crossing frames, 15,128
venue-interior frames, 2,346 seated frames and 0.058 ms per simulated frame over 120 s.

| Watercraft | 30 | Boats, fishing, houseboats |
| Wheels and vehicle parts | 22 | Build pieces, not traffic |
| Rail infrastructure | 8 | Track, signals, stations — not vehicles |
| Watercraft support | 8 | Docks and cargo props |
| Service and emergency | 6 | Service vehicles |
| Commercial | 5 | Delivery, garbage, trucks |
| Karts | 5 | Explicitly **not** everyday traffic |
| Passenger cars | 3 | Hatchback, sedan, sedan sport |
| SUVs and vans | 3 | SUV, luxury SUV, van |
| Sports and performance | 2 | Placeable, not ambient traffic |
| Taxis | 1 | Yellow cab model |

`trafficGroups` is limited to `passenger_cars`, `suv_vans`, `commercial` and `taxis`. Karts, toy-style
models, rail infrastructure, watercraft and parts can be placed in builds but are never spawned as
ambient traffic. There is no separate Toy Car kit in the available collection; the car kit's karts are
the only toy-scale vehicles and are excluded from traffic.

The Build panel shows the vehicle group as filter chips, and the asset gallery has vehicle group
filters with the same labels.

## 7. Vehicles in the world

The live city uses procedural meshes to match LoftLife's visual identity; imported vehicles remain
placeable catalogue assets. This is deliberate: mixing the low-poly Kenney cars into moving traffic
would break the consistent look of the city's hand-built vehicles.

- **16 moving cars** across the major avenue and diagonal corridors: sedans, SUVs, coupes and four
  ambient taxis. They accelerate, brake at signals, stop for kerbside pauses and resume. The
  server-owned hailable taxi is a separate transit vehicle, not one of these background cars.
- **4 LoftLine buses** on 15th Street and M Street, with destination signs and kerbside stops.
- **2 elevated metro trains**, three cars each, on the 16th Street viaduct with station dwell.
- **6 parked cars** along 15th Street kerbs, so private vehicles exist without pretending every car is
  driven.
- Traffic signals are phase-driven and shared with the pedestrian crossing logic.

The existing transit system remains authoritative and is **not** replaced:

- autonomous taxis cruise, pause near players and drive hired riders to 15 taxi destinations;
- buses and trains follow scheduled routes with server-assigned seats;
- passengers share a vehicle when boarding the same line;
- travel transitions hide loading between locations;
- **players are never required to work as taxi or bus drivers.**

Vehicle feedback is visual: headlights, brake lights, turn indicators, destination signs and traffic
signals. **No engine or horn audio was added**, because the project has no licensed vehicle sound
set and inventing one would break the established audio direction. The transit UI keeps its existing
boarding and arrival cues.

## 8. Vehicle interaction and future driving

The current game architecture supports **passenger** transit, not free driving. The integration keeps
that boundary:

- taxi hailing, bus boarding, rail boarding and disembarking use the existing server-owned transit
  flow;
- `enter_vehicle` / `exit_vehicle` poses and seating positions exist on the rig;
- the vehicle catalogue records group, scale and traffic eligibility, so a future drivable vehicle can
  reuse the model, seat and animation metadata;
- no fake steering, collision or fuel system was invented.

A future driving pass still needs vehicle physics, server authority for vehicle state, camera
behaviour, collision response and multiplayer interpolation. Those are intentionally out of scope
here.

## 9. Performance

- **Two-tier crowd LOD** with hard caps per quality (see §5); hidden agents cost nothing.
- **Population cap** independent of city size; agents are recycled, never all simulated.
- **Shared animation function** for player and NPC rigs; no per-agent animation clips or mixers.
- **Model caching** through `kenneyRuntime` (deduped loads, LRU eviction, pending rebuild).
- **Lazy loading**: distant Kenney characters are only requested when an agent reaches the prop tier;
  the first 12 pedestrian models are warmed in the background.
- **Pooled rigs**: at most 10 rig instances exist; the farthest is reclaimed when the budget is full.
- **No extra network traffic**: NPCs are local and deterministic; only players' animation states are
  sent, at the existing movement rate.
- **Mobile**: the renderer caps pixel ratio, disables shadows on low quality and applies the low/medium
  crowd budget on touch devices.

Not implemented: `InstancedMesh` for the static pedestrian tier, per-model LODs and Draco/meshopt
compression. The current prop tier is one draw call per visible pedestrian; with a 22-prop cap this is
acceptable, but instancing is the next optimisation if the cap grows.

## 11. Verification

Automated:

```bash
npm run lint          # tsc --noEmit
npm test              # 208 checks, including animation, crowd and library tests
npm run check:people  # 120 s real-city crowd simulation
npm run build         # production build
```

New tests:

- `server/test/animation.test.mjs` — vocabulary, server normalization, gait, seating and stride.
- `server/test/city-crowd.test.mjs` — sidewalk rings, crossings, roles, reactions, LOD, recycling.
- `server/test/character-vehicle-libraries.test.mjs` — role separation, animation sets, vehicle groups
  and traffic eligibility.

In-game checks (desktop browser against the running dev server):

- signed in and entered TheCity at the Grand Hotel;
- fast-travelled to the Community Center and Market Hall;
- confirmed the pedestrian crowd renders with animated LoftLife rigs and varied outfits;
- confirmed a LoftLine bus stopped at the 15th Street shelter with a second vehicle behind it;
- confirmed Settings exposes Low/Medium/High and the crowd tier is wired to the selected quality
  (the renderer's resolution/shadow change takes effect on the next world entry);
- found no crowd, GLB-loading or world-build console exceptions; the only console entries were a
  favicon 404 and one transient guest-auth 503 during sign-in.

Not checked here: a physical phone/tablet, a second simultaneous player on another device, a live
low→high quality switch on a running world, and production R2 delivery. The automated multi-client
tests cover server state; they do not replace a two-device visual acceptance run.

## 12. Storage and metadata

- Catalogue metadata and integration code live in GitHub.
- Production GLBs, textures and previews are intended for Cloudflare R2 under `catalog/v1/`; the client
  resolves them through `VITE_ASSET_BASE_URL`.
- The upload was **not executed** here because `CATALOG_R2_BUCKET`, `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and
  `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` are not configured. The script is dry-run verified. Without the variable the
  build works but bundles the binary assets and warns.
- No credentials or bucket URLs are committed.

## 13. Known limitations

1. **No rigged/animated Kenney characters exist in the available collection.** Imported characters are
   static; all movement comes from the LoftLife rig.
2. **Toon and Animated character kits could not be inspected** because they are absent from the source
   collection; see §2.
3. **Distant NPCs do not animate.** They bob slightly while moving and face their travel direction;
   the static GLB has no skeleton to animate.
4. **No free driving.** Passenger transit is complete; vehicle physics and server authority are future
   work.
5. **Traffic is concentrated on major corridors** (M Street and selected avenues), not every
   residential street.
6. **Watercraft and rail stock are catalogue assets**, not a working harbour or drivable railway.
7. **No true instancing** for the distant crowd; caps keep the cost bounded.
8. **R2 upload is blocked** on credentials, and the Neon seed still lacks `DATABASE_URL`.
9. **No physical-device performance run** was possible in this environment; mobile budgets are applied
   from the device class but were not measured on hardware.
